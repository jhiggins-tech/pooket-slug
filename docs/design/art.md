# Generated art: the look

How generated art is made for Pooket Slug, so every new background matches the ones before it. The owner picked the look from the art showcase on 2026-10-17 (the gen-art-look item: https://claude.ai/artifact/7KaagiXRYxbuhq5rodNx18); the model it was made with has since changed (2026-10-21: Qwen Image 2.1 Turbo for every image, below).

**Read this when** you're making a background plate, animating a sprite, or making any art with an image or video model.

Up: [Design](README.md)

## Background plates
- **Model: Qwen Image 2.1 Turbo** on OpenRouter (`qwen/qwen-image-2.1-turbo`), about $0.02 an image, for every image from now on, new plates and edits alike (owner, 2026-10-21, spike-qwen-edit: "Turbo with annotations is going to be our go-to model from now on, goodbye seedream"). Changes to a picked painting are marked on it, as below. Seedream 5 Flash (`bytedance-seed/seedream-5-0-flash`), the model from 2026-10-17 to 2026-10-21, made every plate before then.
- **Prompt only.** A new plate is made from its prompt alone, with no reference picture. A hand-drawn reference is made only when a plate's prompt doesn't quite do it.
- **The style line**, put in front of every plate's own prompt:
  > Pixel art in the style of 1990s Neo Geo arcade run-and-gun games like Metal Slug: hand-placed pixels, bold dark outlines, rich three- or four-tone shading, chunky readable shapes. Crisp hard pixel edges, no anti-aliasing, no blur, no text.
- **The plate's prompt** says what the layer shows and nothing else: no ground, vehicles or characters unless they're the point. A layer that sits over others asks for everything around it to be flat magenta `#FF00FF` (a chroma key), with no gradient.
- **Pixels:** the image is cut down to the game's size (the plate's size at 16 pixels a unit, for example 320 × 88 for a far skyline) and to 16 colours, with no dithering, by the vendor repository's `art/plates.py`. Then it's checked in the game, not only on its own.
- Images are made by the vendor repository's CI (the Plates and Showcase workflows), and their spend goes in its usage ledger.
- **In the game** (`Backdrop`, m1-plates-build): a mission's plates are PNGs in `Assets/Resources/Plates/<mission id>/` (`mission-1`, `mission-2`), renamed `.bytes`, one strip per layer: `sky-0`, `sky-1`, … then `far-…` and `mid-…`, numbered along the map. A strip's plates are laid end to end and repeat, and any width works; the height is the layer's. The layers, back to front: **sky** (moves 0.95 of the camera, from the screen's bottom, a full screen tall: 180 pixels), **far** (0.85, its bottom 1.2 units up) and **mid** (0.6, 1 unit up), with the sky gradient behind them all. A mission can stand its far and mid plates higher (`MissionDef.PlatesStand`, units up: Mission two's are 2). A layer with no plates is drawn in code, as before.
- **The cutscenes use them too:** the motor pool's set is built from Mission one's `sky-0`, `far-2` and `mid-6` (`CutsceneBackdrops.PaintedMotorPool`), so renumbering those plates changes the set; without them it falls back to a drawn one.

## The house's room plates
The house's outer rooms are their own screens, each one behind a door
([the lobby](lobby.md#the-outer-rooms)). Each is a room plate, made like a
background plate but standing in for a whole screen, and the work in progress
is in [plates](lobby/plates/). To add one, follow [the workflow](lobby/rooms.md).

- **Model:** Qwen Image 2.1 Turbo, as for every image now. It is not in OpenRouter's public chat model list: find it with `GET /api/v1/images/models/qwen/qwen-image-2.1-turbo/endpoints` (all image models: `GET /api/v1/images/models`).
- **The endpoint** is `POST /api/v1/images`, not `/chat/completions`: an image model on the chat endpoint is a 404 saying so, and the vendor repository's `art/showcase.py` falls back to `/api/v1/images` on that 404. Sizes go in `"size": "WxH"`; each `input_references` entry is `{"type": "image_url", "image_url": {"url": "data:image/png;base64,…"}}`, never a bare string (the vendor repository's `docs/openrouter-media.md`, section 3.1).
- **The recipe is the blocks recipe** (owner, 2026-10-22; the six rooms after the kitchen were made with it, commit c2f1ed0). Step by step, in [rooms.md](lobby/rooms.md#2-paint-it):
  1. **The layout diagram.** `python3 -I tools/room_ref.py <out.svg> <room> --blocks` (this repository's `tools/room_ref.py`; the rooms' boxes are in its `ROOMS`). It draws the room as flat, labelled blocks on a plain wall: no furniture art at all, structure kept only where the geometry matters (a stair's treads, a dome, a hole as a gap in the floor, a door as an opening). The model is told where each thing goes and how big it is, not what it looks like.
  2. **The den's and the loft's finished plates as the style guides**, alongside the diagram. They carry the look (the pixels, the lighting, the palette); the diagram carries the layout. This replaced the swatch.
  3. **The scale is in the diagram.** The soldier is stamped four times along the floor and a chip spells it out ("the soldier: 22 px tall", "the door: 37 px, under twice him"), because the model reads the text in a reference. Without the cue it inflates everything to fill the frame (doors near 80 px against the 22 px soldier).
  4. **Generate at the room's own aspect** (see the size floor below), take several, pick one, patch it with an edit, cut it to the game's size.
- **The kitchen was made with the older two-reference recipe** (2026-10-22, before the blocks): a layout picture cut out of the house map, and a swatch of the game's own pixels upscaled so the pixel technique showed, with `room_ref.py --gen` as the layout (labels and scale note kept, soldier stamped four times, no blue screen outline). Its plate is signed off and stays. The notes below on the reference's labels and on literal copying were learned on it, and they still hold for the blocks diagram.
- **The model copies a reference too literally.** One kitchen take repainted the diagram almost 1:1 ([take4](lobby/plates/wip/take4-kitchen.png)): flat untextured walls, and all four soldier scale markers painted in as characters. Saying the little soldier figures are scale markers only, do not paint them, helped and was ignored about half the time — it isn't deterministic. The fix for an otherwise good take with phantom soldiers is an edit pass ([Editing a picked painting](#editing-a-picked-painting)).
- **The size floor.** The provider wants at least 512 × 512, so a plate is generated larger than the game's size and cut down to the game's size and 64 colours afterwards. Generate at the room's own aspect — the 420 × 180 kitchen at 1260 × 540 (the `size` field, `"1260x540"`) — not at a stock one: the kitchen's takes at 1536 × 768 were the bloated ones.
- **Take several, pick one, patch it.** With the same prompt and references, the kitchen's takes ranged from excellent to compressed, flat and bloated. Generate three to five takes of a room, pick the best, and fix what's wrong with an edit rather than regen and hope. What gets checked in is the game cut of the pick — ffmpeg, a median cut to 64 colours, area-scaled to the room's art size — as `Assets/Resources/Den/room-<name>.bytes`.
- **Moderation.** The provider's moderation blocks some *combinations*, not any single part: the style line above passes on its own, but adding "16-bit" to it is blocked, and a layout hint that says "paint it in the style of the second picture" is blocked too. Keep the style line exactly as it is, and keep the hint plain: "The first picture is a layout diagram for the room: where each thing goes."
- **The palette.** Cut the plate down with a **median cut**, not by keeping the most common colours: the paintings have ramps of light, and the commonest-colours cut crushes a ramp into one muddy band.

## Editing a picked painting
To change one part of a painting that's already picked (a room, a prop), edit it rather than make a new take: mark the part with a red circle or box and say what goes there. Tried on the loft's wintergarden (spike-qwen-edit, 2026-10-21; page https://claude.ai/artifact/RLupVt9zB3RAQU1tdHfYHw): eight takes, then six more on Turbo, about $0.50 in all.
- **Model: Qwen Image 2.1 Turbo** on OpenRouter (`qwen/qwen-image-2.1-turbo`), about $0.02 an image and 30 to 50 seconds a call. It takes the whole painting as a reference, at full size, and changes what it's told to. Pro (`qwen/qwen-image-2.1-pro`, about $0.05) did no better here and missed a detail Turbo got (the lemons), so Turbo is the default. Both are only on `POST /api/v1/images`: each `input_references` entry is an object (`{"type": "image_url", "image_url": {"url": "data:…"}}`), not a string; 21:9 isn't an `aspect_ratio` they take, but `"size": "3024x1296"` came back at exactly that size. The vendor repository's `art/qwen_edit.py` (branch `claude/qwen-edit`) makes the calls.
- **Mark the part, or name it.** A red circle or box round the part, with "change only what's inside the red circle" in the prompt, worked, and the mark was gone from the answer. Naming the place in words ("the glass room between the server cupboard and the red vending machine") worked about as well. With a mark, the room's frame stayed put more often.
- **Paste the answer back through a mask.** Qwen repaints the whole picture: a fifth of the pixels outside the mark shift a little, invisible at a glance but not identical. Keep only the marked part, pasted back through a mask of it, so the rest of the plate stays exactly as picked.
- **It holds the look better than a fill edit.** Seedream's fill edit (a 16:9 crop, the part painted out) leaves the rest exact but paints softer, scales the contents up and wandered from the brief (pink roses, sliding doors). Qwen kept the hard pixel look and the scale. Pro missed one detail in all six of its takes (no lemons on the lemon tree), so check the brief item by item.
- **Check it at game size in the plate's own colours:** cut it down and map it to the picked plate's palette, not a fresh 64 colours. A fresh palette fitted to the new warm room let the vending machine's red go orange.

The kitchen's room plate leant on these more (2026-10-22, [the plates](lobby/plates/)):

- **Strays come out in an edit, not a regen.** The kitchen's best take had three phantom soldiers in it (from the reference's scale markers, above); one edit pass — "Remove the three small pixel-art soldier figures... Fill the places where they stood... Change nothing else" — removed them and left the rest of the plate intact, proportions and lighting held. Regenerating to lose them would have thrown the take away.
- **A hero prop too plain in the reference is fixed by a free-imagining edit.** The reference SVG's coffee machine was crude, so instead of regenerating, the plate was edited with the machine marked in a red rectangle (ffmpeg `drawbox`) and "Replace only what is inside the red rectangle with a beautiful, fancy barista espresso coffee machine, imagined freely". The model imagined a brass machine that fit the lighting. A second, word-only edit then refined it — "Change only the brass espresso coffee machine between the wooden bench and the fridge. Put it standing on a small wooden table... shown straight on... slightly smaller... Change nothing else" — and moved nothing else. Rule of thumb: **mark in red** for swaps in busy areas, **words only** for clear standalone props.


## Sprites
Sprites and their animations are hand-made, in `ArtData` and the per-crewmate, per-mission and per-tabk art files beside it (`KcajArt`, `CreameryArt` and the rest), and stay that way. Image models can't draw a usable sprite or sprite sheet at the game's size (gen-sprites, 2026-10-18). A video model can animate a sprite we drew, though: the workflow below was locked in on 2026-10-18 (sprite-model-research: https://claude.ai/artifact/LrvdRFdJF6R4qnVvWewhyw) and audited on the sprite-anim-pipeline card.

**When to use it:** only for a sprite or an animation the hand-made art is failing at, such as a movement in a cutscene that's hard to animate by hand. The owner picks each one and promotes it to this workflow on the Roadmap; nothing moves over on its own (owner, 2026-10-18).

### The workflow
1. **Start frame:** the hand-made frame, scaled up by a whole number on flat magenta `#FF00FF`, 16:9, nothing else in the picture.
   - *One sprite in the clip* (about 20×) for detailed things and big moves: a hero, a boss, a tabk.
   - *Several sprites in one clip* (about 12×, side by side, evenly spaced) for less detailed things with subtler moves, such as goons and props. One clip animates them all, each in its own spot.
2. **The clip:** Wan 3.0 (`alibaba/wan-3.0`) through OpenRouter's `POST /api/v1/videos`, the start frame as `frame_images` (`first_frame`), 480p, 3 to 5 seconds, about $0.05 a second. The prompt says what moves and holds the rest still: "Side-on 2D pixel-art game sprite. The character [does the move]. Exact same pixel-art style, colours and outline, crisp square pixels. Fixed camera, flat magenta background."
   - **A loop** (a run, an idle): give the start frame as the `last_frame` too.
   - **A tween** (a pose change, a death, a throw, an explosion): give a different hand-made `last_frame` and the model fills in the middle.
   - **Effects:** also try Veo 3.1 Lite (`google/veo-3.1-lite`, 720p only, 4 s at least, `generate_audio: false`), whose explosion had more sparks.
3. **Frames:** `ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -vf fps=12 frames/f%03d.png`.
4. **Back to game pixels:** `recut.py frames START.png COLUMN OUT [COLUMNS]` cuts one sprite out of the clip (COLUMN of COLUMNS, for a clip of several). It keys the magenta out hard and scales so the first frame is the sprite's own height, with its feet on the same row. It takes one sample at the centre of each game pixel, and never averages, which would leave a magenta halo. Then it maps every pixel to the start frame's palette. It writes a strip of frames and a 4× GIF.
5. **A person finishes it:** pick a clean run of 4 to 8 frames, tidy stray pixels, and show it on the card's page beside the hand-made one. Only what the owner picks goes into `ArtData`.

### Where the tools are
The vendor repository's `art/sprite-anim/`: `video.py` (sends the takes in a JSON file, polls, and downloads the clips; a take already made isn't paid for again) and `recut.py`, with the workflow `sprite-anim.yml`, publishing to the `sprite-anim` release. The workflow runs only when started by hand, with the parameter file's path, so a push never starts a paid run. Clips are made by the vendor's CI and logged in its usage ledger, like every generation.

### Watch out for
- **The cut assumes the first frame is the whole sprite.** A tween that starts small, such as a grenade that becomes an explosion, needs the scale given rather than measured. `recut.py` also re-centres every frame on its own width, which takes out a move's travel (a lunge, a jump); keep the feet's x if the travel matters.
- **The frame rate is 12 a second,** with some noisy frames: plan to throw most of them away.
- **Other models:** HeyGen Video 1 flashes red and yellow frames; Grok Imagine 1.5 Lite is soft and noisy; Hailuo 3 Max takes only one keyframe, so it can't loop or tween.
- **A failed or retried clip is a new paid job:** change the prompt, don't resend it blindly.

## The title logo
Picked by the owner on 2026-10-18 (the start-art item: https://claude.ai/artifact/Xygy3vrT2fFQdav1WFPa14) for the splash screen: gold pixel lettering, POOKET SLUG on one line, with a small gold tabk sitting above the gap between the words, in the manner of an SNES title screen.
- **The file:** `/mnt/project-files/concepts/start-art/logo-c1.png` (2136 × 496, its black keyed out to transparency), cut from take `c-1.png` (2560 × 1440 on black). It was made with Seedream 5 Flash, prompt only.
- **On screen:** it sits in the upper two thirds over the screen's own background (a gentle colour ramp and a vignette, drawn by the game), with START and SETTINGS below it. The page tried it over a royal-blue ramp, `#060b3a` at the top to `#2b3aa8` at the bottom.
- **Keying:** the dark outline around the letters went with the black, so give the logo a one-pixel dark outline or a soft drop shadow if it looks thin on the ramp.
- **Built** (start-screen, 2026-10-18): `Ui/TitleView.cs`. The logo is `Assets/Resources/Title/logo.bytes`, a PNG made by `tools/title_logo.py`: scaled to 1280 wide with a 3-pixel dark outline and a soft shadow baked in, shown 440 of the canvas's 360-high units wide. The ramp is that royal blue, with a vignette darkening the corners by up to 60%; the splash fades up from black once a visit.
- **Its animation** (title-logo-animation, the owner's picks A2, H2 and G3, 2026-10-19; the options: https://claude.ai/artifact/CSmHaZPzP9B14dmK76LCXK): the logo is cut into its ten letters and the tabk (`tools/title_logo.py` writes them to `parts.bytes` with their shadows and gold masks, `layout.txt` says where each goes). **In:** the tabk drives in from the left along the top of the word to an engine turning over and revving, each letter springing up as its nose passes; it stops and recoils with a cannon shot and a short shake, the title music starts, and S-L-U-G pop up. START and SETTINGS fade in once it's landed; a tap skips to the end (the music starts at once). **Hover:** the logo bobs gently while its shadow stays put, darkening as the logo dips. **Glint:** a band of light sweeps over the gold as it lands and every six seconds, ending in a sparkle off the barrel's tip. The hover and glint are silent. The timings are `Core/Title/TitleMotion.cs`; the drawing `Ui/TitleLogoView.cs`.
